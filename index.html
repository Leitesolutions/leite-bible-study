Study notes

Go a little deeper.

Every guide starts with the passage itself and offers questions for reflection.

GOSPEL · 6 MIN READ

Reading the Gospel of Mark

Mark opens with movement: a wilderness voice, a baptism, and a call to follow. As you read, notice how people respond to Jesus and how the disciples’ understanding changes. The Gospel invites its readers to consider who Jesus is and what following him costs.

Read: Mark 1:1–15 · Ask: What words or actions introduce Jesus here?

PRAYER · 5 MIN READ

Praying with the Psalms

The Psalms give language to the full range of human experience. Some are songs of praise; others are cries for help or prayers of confession. Their honesty reminds readers that prayer can begin with what is real, bringing both gratitude and pain before God.

Read: Psalm 13 · Ask: Where does the psalm turn, and what changes?

WISDOM · 5 MIN READ

Wisdom in everyday choices

Proverbs gathers short teachings about living wisely in community. Wisdom here is practical: listen before speaking, seek understanding, treat others justly, and recognize that choices shape character over time. These sayings invite reflection rather than offering a mechanical formula for every situation.

Read: Proverbs 3:1–12 · Ask: What kind of trust does this passage describe?

OVERVIEW · 7 MIN READ

The Bible’s big story

The Bible is a collection of writings from different times and settings: law, history, poetry, prophecy, Gospels, letters, and more. Many readers trace a broad story through creation, covenant, Israel, Jesus, the early church, and the hope of renewal. Each book has its own voice and context.

Read: Luke 24:13–35 · Ask: How does understanding the wider story change what you notice?

REFLECTION · 6 MIN READ

Faith and honest questions

Scripture includes people who lament, protest, wonder, and wait. The book of Job asks difficult questions about suffering without reducing them to easy answers. Lamentations gives grief its own voice. A thoughtful study can hold questions open while listening carefully to the text and to one another.

Read: Job 3:1–10 · Ask: What does this passage allow the speaker to say?

PRACTICE · 5 MIN READ

Loving your neighbour

In the parable of the Good Samaritan, Jesus answers a question about who counts as a neighbour with a story about costly compassion. The listener is invited to notice not only who receives care, but who chooses to cross a boundary and offer it.

Read: Luke 10:25–37 · Ask: What does compassion look like in this story?
